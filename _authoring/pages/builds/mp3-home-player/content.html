2001 · University Project

MP3 Home Player

A standalone home audio unit that connected to a stereo system and played WAV, MIDI and MP3 files.

Why I made it

I wanted a challenging final-year university project that brought together the different parts of my Computer Science and Networking degree. I loved music, and MP3s were becoming hugely popular. Why not build a physical MP3 player I could control over a network?

The player

The unit had internal storage, standard playback controls and a menu-driven interface. A companion app handled remote operation, library management and MP3 transfer across a network.

Making it work

I had to get the LCD working over a serial connection, program the keypad, write C software on FreeBSD and build a Windows control app in Visual Basic. Music moved over FTP, and the app handled remote control and library management. Then I had to make the hardware and software work together as one physical device.

The result was a self-contained home music system that brought network-managed digital audio playback to a stereo setup.

What happened

I used the player myself, and several people asked me to build one for them. I think there might have been a small business in it, but I'd just finished university, had little money and was focused on getting a proper job. I didn't pursue it commercially.

Looking back

I'm proud when I see the original photo. I was trying things I hadn't done before just to see whether I could make them work. Building, learning, experimenting and making something tangible were part of why I fell in love with computers and coding. And it was all about music, which I loved.

It was awesome.
